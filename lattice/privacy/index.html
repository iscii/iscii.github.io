Lattice privacy policy

Last updated 11 November 2026.

Lattice keeps your data on your iPhone. If sync is set up, it also keeps a copy on a private server run by the developer. Lattice has no accounts and no ads, and it does not sell your data.

What Lattice stores

Your tasks, projects, habits, journal entries and voice recordings are saved in an encrypted database on your iPhone, inside the app. Lattice also keeps a local log of which features are used (for example, "a task was moved"), which never contains your text and is not synced. Deleting the app deletes what is on your iPhone; it does not delete a copy already synced to the server (see below).

Apple Calendar

If you allow calendar access, Lattice reads and writes events through iOS. Your events stay in Apple Calendar. Lattice also saves a copy of events from the calendars you turn on, as described under Calendar events below.

Microphone

If you record a voice clip in the journal, Lattice uses the microphone only while you record. Recordings are saved on your iPhone and, if sync is set up, copied to the server.

Sync and the Lattice server

Sync keeps a copy of your data on a server run by the developer, so it can be kept safe and used from other tools. It works both ways: changes made on the server, such as by the assistant described below, come back to your iPhone. In this version sync is set up only on the developer's own device; on any other install it sends nothing.

What is sent: your tasks, projects, habits, goals, templates, check-ins, workouts, journal entries, voice recordings and their transcripts, calendar events (see below), and your Lattice settings. Health data from Oura and your local usage log are not sent.

Calendar events: events from the calendars you have turned on in Lattice, from 30 days ago to 90 days ahead, are saved in Lattice's database and synced. Each saved event holds its source (Apple or Google), its calendar's id and name, an identifier, its title, notes, location, start and end times, whether it is all-day, its repeat details and when it was last seen. An event that is deleted, moves out of that window, or belongs to a calendar you turn off or disconnect is marked as removed; older copies stay stored until they are purged. There is currently no setting to turn off event storage other than turning off sync, which stops events being sent.

How it is protected: sync travels over an encrypted connection on a private network. On the server it is stored on an encrypted disk, access needs a per-device token, and backups are encrypted before they are copied to the developer's own storage.

Who can read and change it: the developer. The developer can also connect the server to an AI assistant (Claude, by Anthropic) so it can read the synced data when asked and, where the developer has allowed it, add or change tasks, habit logs, check-ins, journal entries and notes, which then sync back to the iPhone. Its edits are merged with yours rather than replacing them. That connection needs a sign-in and is read-only. It reaches the server through Cloudflare, whose network decrypts the traffic in transit to route and protect it, so what the assistant reads passes through Cloudflare, and is then sent to Anthropic to answer the request.

Turning it off: Settings > Data > Sync > Turn off stops anything further being sent or received. It does not delete the copy on the server; to have that deleted, use the contact below.

Diagnostics

Builds set up for testing send diagnostic events to the developer's server over the same private network: which screens and actions were used, ids, states, counts, screen positions, and error messages. They are designed never to include titles, notes or journal text. They are used only to find and fix bugs.

Google Calendar

Google Calendar is off until you turn it on in Settings and sign in with Google. Lattice then asks for:

  • Your Google account's email address and basic profile, to show which account is connected.
  • Access to your Google Calendar (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar), to list your calendars, show their events on Lattice's timeline, and create, edit and delete events when you ask it to.

How this data is handled:

  • Requests go directly from your iPhone to Google. With sync off, which is every install other than the developer's own, your Google data is not sent to the developer or anyone else.
  • With sync on, events from the Google calendars you have turned on are stored on the server as described under Sync, and can be read by the AI assistant described there, through Cloudflare.
  • Events are saved on your iPhone, from 30 days ago to 90 days ahead, so the calendar, its widget and sync can use them.
  • Your Google sign-in is kept in the iOS Keychain by Google's sign-in library.
  • Lattice does not use Google data for advertising, does not sell it, and does not use it to train AI or machine-learning models. It does not transfer it to anyone, except as described under Sync on the developer's own device.
  • You can disconnect at any time: Settings > Google Calendar > Sign out. You can also remove Lattice's access from your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Lattice's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

App updates

Lattice checks for app updates from Expo's update service, which receives standard technical information (such as the app version and platform) to deliver them. It receives none of your content.

Changes

If Lattice adds another feature that sends your data off your iPhone, or changes what sync sends, this policy will be updated before that change ships.
